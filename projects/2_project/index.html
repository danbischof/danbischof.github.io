Democratic Norms

Studying which role social norms play for democracies

This project speaks to literature on social norms and democratic stability, research on citizens’ assemblies and legitimacy, and studies of opinion leadership in political behavior.

While democracy research has examined citizens’ abstract support and institutional trust, the social underpinnings—how individuals’ perceptions of what others do (descriptive norms) and ought to do (injunctive norms) shape democratic attitudes and behaviors—remain underexplored.

How we addressed it

  • Cross‑national surveys: Measured descriptive and injunctive democratic norms across 14 countries, using nationally representative data covering 31 % of the world’s population.
  • Vignette experiments: Embedded manipulated norm messages in large‑scale survey experiments (N > 7,000) to test causal effects on democratic support, protest tolerance, and hate‑crime justification.
  • Policy‑forum simulations: Designed conjoint experiments mirroring citizens’ assemblies to assess the influence of local opinion leaders on policy proposal support.

Key Findings

  1. Strong but abstract norms: Citizens overestimate peer support for democracy in the abstract, but this perceived consensus weakens when evaluating specific democratic rights.
  2. Norms shape tolerance: Higher perceived descriptive norms increase tolerance for real‑world protest groups, whereas injunctive norms have conditional effects depending on group identity.
  3. Opinion leader cues: Local opinion leaders significantly amplify policy‑proposal support when their positions align with prevailing norms and shared social identity.
  4. Risk of oversimplification: Framing democratic values as a monolithic consensus can obscure pluralistic interpretations and marginalize minority viewpoints.

Implications

For practice: Civic education and participatory forums should foster nuanced norm discussions to guard against false consensus and democratic complacency. Opinion‑leader interventions can boost legitimacy but must avoid reinforcing echo chambers.

For social science: Incorporating social‑norms theory enriches democracy research, urging scholars to refine measures of descriptive and injunctive norms and examine their heterogeneous effects across contexts and groups.
